# 01. Problem Statement

*All findings below are hypotheses, not validated research.*

## Context

India has a very large blue-collar workforce, and much of its hiring is informal: word of mouth, contractors, and messaging groups. LinkedIn is built around professional identity and the résumé, which assume formal credentials. The opportunity is to ask what "professional identity" means for someone whose skill is practical and demonstrable.

## Problem statement

Skilled workers with little formal education struggle to find trustworthy, fairly paid, local work, and to prove their ability to employers who don't know them. Employers, in turn, struggle to tell who is reliable and skilled. As a result, good workers and good jobs miss each other.

## Goal

Help blue-collar workers discover and secure suitable, trustworthy jobs in their locality.

## Primary segment (selected)

**Skilled trades, starting with electricians.**

Why this segment first:
1. Skill is visible and can be shown, for example a photo of finished wiring.
2. Work is often daily-wage or contractual, so wage clarity matters.
3. There is a clear career ladder: helper → technician → contractor.
4. Demand is local, which makes a one-city pilot feasible.
5. It tests the core bet (skills proof) most directly.

Considered and deferred: factory and warehouse shift work, and domestic and household help. Domestic work likely carries higher personal-safety risk and needs stronger protections before launch.

## Proto-persona (assumption-based)

**Murugan, 27.** Tamil-speaking, left school before Class 10, about six years in electrical work, now an independent wireman. Budget Android phone, uses WhatsApp and YouTube. Finds work through one contractor and word of mouth.
- **Wants:** steady work, fair pay, a move up to contractor.
- **Fears:** unpaid work, fake jobs, being cheated on wages.

## Starting hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Type |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Language barriers stop workers from using job apps | Entry barrier |
| H2 | Complicated interfaces drive early drop-off | Entry barrier |
| H3 | Workers can't prove practical skills in a form employers trust | Conversion barrier |
| H4 | Workers see few relevant local jobs | Discovery |
| H5 | Wages and working conditions are often unclear | Trust |
| H6 *(added)* | Small employers will post clear wages and respond to applicants | Supply side |
| H7 *(added)* | Employers will confirm starts and give vouches | Measurement and growth loop |

H6 and H7 are added on purpose: this is a two-sided marketplace, and the original hypotheses only covered the worker side.

## Goals and non-goals

**Goals (MVP pilot):** local skill-matched job discovery, clear wage and employer information, optional proof of work, visible application status, light trust features.

**Non-goals (MVP):** courses and upskilling, wage benchmarking, in-app chat, payments, social feed, other trades and cities.

## Open questions

- Will workers actually record proof, or will it feel like effort with no reward?
- Is voice input accurate enough across dialects to be useful?
- Can employer confirmation of "job started" be collected reliably?
- Is a standalone app the right form, or should it start inside WhatsApp?
